Our story

From a camp horse to a marketplace.

The Adela Rose started with a horse, a farm and a tractor bought at an online auction. It has been about making things work better ever since.

The start

A camp horse named Mr T

At 14 I started riding Mr T, a YMCA camp horse. I went on to study finance at The Ohio State University, and the Y offered me a full-time job before I even graduated. I took it for the benefits, and it became a 20-year career in nonprofit leadership.

Ohio to Michigan

Camp executive, and Mr T becomes mine

A business degree got me fast-tracked to executive director of a YMCA camp in Ohio, and then recruited to run a camp for the YMCA of Grand Rapids. Mr T was mine by then. I bought him and brought him with me. I rode him all over Michigan and in the mountains of Tennessee. He has passed, but he gave me an amazing life, and he is part of this company.

The farm

A small farm in Caledonia

I had always wanted a farm. We bought a small one in Caledonia and moved Mr T and the other horses there. We wanted to eat healthy, so I decided to raise beef cattle.

The herd

Bringing Thor home

I found Thor because of his bloodlines. They were rare and imported, exactly the new genetics I wanted for the herd, so I drove to the Upper Peninsula to bring him home. He was just a young bull calf, and so friendly that my mom used to brush him. Powerful, yet calm and steady, he had the temperament I wanted in the herd, and it is the temperament I want in this company. I also traveled to New Hampshire and around Michigan for registered breeding stock, and bought two cows with heifer calves at their sides. The herd was off and running.

The name

Adela Rose

To register the cattle I needed a farm name. We had a new daughter, Adela Rose, and my best friend Tim said it was easy: The Adela Rose Farm. It was perfect, and it is still our name.

The tractor

Five generators and a lesson

A farm needs a tractor, and I found one at an online auction. Then I needed a generator and bid on a few. They went so cheap I ended up with five. I sold them on Craigslist within a week and tripled my money. That was the spark.

2009

A resale business, run from the farm

Around 2009 or 2010 I started buying inventory that sold for less than it was worth and reselling it on eBay, Amazon and Craigslist, all under The Adela Rose Farm. The farm’s outbuildings stored the inventory and shipped it directly. When the auto industry crashed I bought liquidation parts, and later a pool shop, a hardware store and an electrical company’s inventory. It grew into a full-time business.

2013

M37 Auction

In 2013 we opened a brick-and-mortar location on M-37 in Caledonia, and that became M37 Auction. Since then I have built and designed the systems behind a busy online auction marketplace.

Today

Technology that creates efficiencies

Everything we learned running a real business now goes into helping other businesses: automated phone answering, dashboards, a CRM, social media that runs on schedule, and websites, in English and Spanish.

Thor · the cookie cow
Why a belted Galloway

You are what you eat.

Dark on both ends, one clean white stripe in the middle. People call them cookie cows, and once you see it you can't unsee it.

The joke has a point. Our beef was grass-fed. Belted Galloways grow a double coat that keeps them warm, so they carry less fat and the beef is leaner. No preventive antibiotics and no growth hormones. Healthy animal, healthy beef.
