How we start

Ask why first. Then measure what matters.

Two habits from a career of building membership and running campaigns, and the way every project here begins.

Why-First

When someone calls or walks in, don’t just tell them what you offer. Ask why they came. At the YMCA, most people assume it is a health club, but one person joins to lose weight, one just moved to town and wants to meet people, one wants the kids to play while they relax, and another wants their child to learn to play. Each needs a different answer, and the same is true in any business. Then capture their contact information, tailor what you say, and follow up. Every inquiry goes into a process, and none gets lost.

What gets measured gets done

We start every project by deciding the business objectives and which metrics will get you the best results, because that is what we measure. Then everyone sees the scores and pulls the same way. At the YMCA, calls, tours, joins and conversion rates were how I knew which TV and radio ads actually worked. At M37Auction, the no-bid rate matters most, because we want our staff to maximize what our clients earn from their sales.

Marketing across every medium

I have planned and managed ad budgets for projects and ongoing campaigns, from membership drives to auctions, across print, TV, radio, social, email, text and the web. We plan it together, and we track what each dollar brings back.

Always improving

Plan, do, study, act, then go around again. Charts and graphs tell us what is working, we fix what is not, and we keep going.

Our story

From a camp horse to a marketplace.

The Adela Rose started with a horse, a farm and a tractor bought at an online auction. It has been about making things work better ever since.

The start

A camp horse named Mr T

At 14 I started riding Mr T, a YMCA camp horse. I went on to study finance at The Ohio State University. Yes, that Ohio State, and yes, I now live in Michigan. I know. Most of my neighbors have forgiven me, and the rest are working on it. The Y offered me a full-time job before I even graduated. I took it for the benefits, and it became a 20-year career in nonprofit leadership.

Ohio to Michigan

Camp executive, and Mr T becomes mine

A business degree got me fast-tracked to executive director of a YMCA camp in Ohio, and then recruited to run a camp for the YMCA of Grand Rapids. Mr T was mine by then. I bought him and brought him with me. I rode him all over Michigan and in the mountains of Tennessee. He has passed, but he gave me an amazing life, and he is part of this company.

The farm

A small farm in Caledonia

I had always wanted a farm. We bought a small one in Caledonia and moved Mr T and the other horses there. We wanted to eat healthy, so I decided to raise beef cattle.

The herd

Bringing Thor home

I found Thor because of his bloodlines. They were rare and imported, exactly the new genetics I wanted for the herd, so I drove to the Upper Peninsula to bring him home. He was just a young bull calf, and so friendly that my mom used to brush him. Powerful, yet calm and steady, he had the temperament I wanted in the herd, and it is the temperament I want in this company. I also traveled to New Hampshire and around Michigan for registered breeding stock, and bought two cows with heifer calves at their sides. The herd was off and running.

The name

Adela Rose

To register the cattle I needed a farm name. We had a new daughter, Adela Rose, and my best friend Tim said it was easy: The Adela Rose Farm. It was perfect, and it is still our name. Here is the funny part. Adela Rose is now a sophomore at the University of Michigan. She is studying kinesiology there. Yes, a Wolverine. I am an Ohio State grad living in Michigan, and my own daughter picked the other team. I have never felt so outnumbered. Go Blue, I guess. (Quietly.)

The tractor

Five generators and a lesson

A farm needs a tractor, and I found one at an online auction. Then I needed a generator and bid on a few. They went so cheap I ended up with five. I sold them on Craigslist within a week and tripled my money. That was the spark.

2009

A resale business, run from the farm

Around 2009 or 2010 I started buying inventory that sold for less than it was worth and reselling it on eBay, Amazon and Craigslist, all under The Adela Rose Farm. The farm’s outbuildings stored the inventory and shipped it directly. When the auto industry crashed I bought liquidation parts, and later a pool shop, a hardware store and an electrical company’s inventory. It grew into a full-time business.

2013

M37Auction

In 2013 we opened a brick-and-mortar location on M-37 in Caledonia, and that became M37Auction. Since then I have built and designed the systems behind a busy online auction marketplace.

Today

Technology that creates efficiencies

Everything we learned running a real business now goes into helping other businesses: automated phone answering, dashboards, a CRM, social media that runs on schedule, and websites, in English and Spanish.

Thor · the cookie cow
Why a belted Galloway

You are what you eat.

Dark on both ends, one clean white stripe in the middle. People call them cookie cows, and once you see it you can't unsee it.

The joke has a point. Our beef was grass-fed. Belted Galloways grow a double coat that keeps them warm, so they carry less fat and the beef is leaner. No preventive antibiotics and no growth hormones. Healthy animal, healthy beef.

The cookie cows

Yes, they really do look like Oreos.

Belted Galloways get called Oreo cows, and our herd never argued. Photographer Joe Aldini heard about ours and asked to come out and photograph them. He makes funny nature pictures, each one a photo with a punchline.

The joke in the big one is on the print itself: You Are What You Eat. A cow that looks like a cookie, face first in a bag of cookies, while her friend gets caught with one in his mouth. Guilty.

And what goes with cookies? Milk. One of our calves sat for the milk mustache.

One fall we cleared out the barn and hung Joe’s prints for the county’s Arts and Eats tour, where artists set up at restaurants and farms around the county. We were a stop. People came to meet the cows and see the art.
